The Criminal Code establishes a fundamental list of crimes and basic regulations regarding criminal justice.
<br><br>
<p align="right">The Kingdom of Zatsunei</p>

<br>

# Title I ― General Provisions

## Chapter 1 ― Scope of Application

### § 1. No penalty before law
No act shall be punished unless it
was defined as a criminal offense by law at the time it was
committed.

### § 2. Territorial validity
**1.** This Criminal Code is applicable to
offenses committed within the territory of the Kingdom of
Zatsunei.

**2.** The Criminal Code applies to Zatsunese land, airspace, and territorial waters.

### § 3. Validity on means of transport in foreign territory
The Criminal Code shall apply to means of transport under Zatsunese protection where such means of transport are identifiable as Zatsunese through visible markings or official documentation.

### § 4. Offenses in foreign territory
**1.** An offense committed in foreign territory against a Zatsunese national may be prosecuted under Zatsunese law when jurisdiction is established under this Code and the offender is subject to Zatsunese authority.

**2.** A Zatsunese national who commits an offense in foreign territory may be prosecuted under this Criminal Code when the person is subject to Zatsunese authority and was not punished in the foreign territory for the offense.

**3.** The Kingdom of Zatsunei may intervene against criminal offenses committed in foreign territory that threaten the security, sovereignty, or interests of Zatsunei.

<br>

## Chapter 2 ― Criminal Liability

### § 5. Definition and properties of an offense
**1.** An offense is an act contrary to law that is defined as punishable under this Criminal Code and for which criminal responsibility may arise.

**2.** An offense shall be evaluated according to the nature of the act, its legal classification, and its commission, including the place and time at which it occurred.

### § 6. Criminal responsibility
Any person who commits an act prohibited by this Criminal Code shall be held criminally responsible, unless otherwise provided by law.

### § 7. Mental incapacity
A person who commits an offense but has been officially deemed mentally incapable of understanding the criminal nature of the act shall not be punished or shall receive a significantly mitigated punishment.

### § 8. Intent and negligence
**1.** An intentional offense is committed when a person acts with knowledge and intent that their conduct will result in the commission of a criminal offense.

**2.** A negligent offense is committed when a person fails to exercise the required level of care, resulting in a criminal offense that is punishable under this Criminal Code, with the possibility of a mitigated sentence depending on the nature of the act.

### § 9. Attempts
Any attempt to commit a criminal offense shall be
punishable in accordance with the nature and classification of
the intended offense, with potential mitigation of sentence.

### § 10. Conspiracy
**1.** A person commits criminal conspiracy by intentionally agreeing with one or more persons to commit a criminal offense.

**2.** Criminal conspiracy shall be punishable by imprisonment for a term not exceeding one-half of the maximum penalty prescribed for the intended offense.

**3.** An attempt or preparatory act shall be punishable in accordance with the provisions established in §§ 9 and 11.
 
### § 11. Preparation
**1.** Preparation is an act of intentionally undertaking actions to facilitate the commission of an offense.

**2.** Preparatory acts are punishable only where this Criminal Code expressly so provides.

**3.** Preparatory acts that escalate into an attempt shall be punished as such.

### § 12. Withdrawal and abandonment
A person who voluntarily withdraws from the commission of a criminal offense and abandons the undertaking in good faith may receive a mitigated sentence or be exempt from punishment.

### § 13. Assistance against crime
**1.** A person who becomes aware of an imminent criminal offense shall make reasonable efforts to report or prevent it when reasonably able to do so.

**2.** A person who, through gross negligence, fails to make a reasonable effort to report or prevent the commission of a criminal offense commits a Misdemeanor Class B.

**3.** A person who withdraws from the commission of a criminal offense in accordance with § 12 and cooperates with the authorities to prevent or resolve the offense may receive further mitigation of punishment or may be exempt from punishment.

### § 14. Forms of offenders
**1.** The following forms of offenders shall be recognized under this Code:
- (a) Principal offender, an offender who directly commits a criminal offense;

- (b) Co-perpetrator, an offender who directly commits a criminal offense together with the principal offender or another co-perpetrator;

- (c) Instigator, an offender who intentionally causes, directs, or persuades another person to commit a criminal offense;

- (d) Accomplice, an offender who intentionally assists, facilitates, or provides the means for the commission or completion of a criminal offense.

### § 15. Corporate and collective criminal responsibility
**1.** A legal person may be held criminally responsible when an offense is committed by a person acting for, representing, or under the control of that legal person.

**2.** The responsibility of a legal person does not remove the responsibility of individuals involved in the offense.

**3.** Individuals who commit, assist, authorize, or fail to prevent an offense remain responsible under this Code.

### § 16. Crimes committed by foreigners
A criminal offense committed by a foreign person may result in their deportation from the territory of the Kingdom of Zatsunei or other appropriate penalties in accordance with this Code.

## Chapter 3 ― Classification and Penalties of Offenses

### § 17. Classification of offenses
**1.** A misdemeanor is a criminal offense of lesser gravity and is classified into two classes:
- (a) Misdemeanor Class A, which is punishable by an enhanced fine, community service, or imprisonment for more than one week but not exceeding two weeks;

- (b) Misdemeanor Class B, which is punishable by a fine, community service, or imprisonment for more than three days but not exceeding one week;

**2.** A felony is a criminal offense of greater gravity and is classified into three classes:
- (a) Felony Class A, which is punishable by a maximum fine, imprisonment for more than five weeks but not exceeding two months, exile, total confiscation, or life imprisonment;
  - (i) Exile, total confiscation, and life imprisonment shall be considered exceptional penalties and shall be imposed only where expressly authorized by this Code.

- (b) Felony Class B, which is punishable by a maximum fine or imprisonment for more than three weeks but not exceeding five weeks.

- (c) Felony Class C, which is punishable by an enhanced fine or imprisonment for more than two weeks but not exceeding three weeks.

### § 18. Penalties
**1.** A fine is a monetary penalty imposed on a person convicted of an offense. A fine may be imposed in the following forms:
- (a) Fine, which shall consist of the payment of 144 gold nuggets.

- (b) Enhanced fine, which shall consist of the payment of 288 gold nuggets.

- (c) Maximum fine, which shall consist of the payment of 576 gold nuggets.

- (d) Where an offender is sentenced to imprisonment and a fine, the fine shall become payable upon release. An offender who is unable to immediately pay the fine shall be granted a period of at least one week but not exceeding three weeks to obtain and pay the required amount.

- (e) Failure to pay the fine within the prescribed period may result in the seizure of property of equivalent value or additional imprisonment, as ordered by a court.

**2.** Community service shall be the performance of unpaid work or services for the benefit of the public, as designated by the authorities.

**3.** Imprisonment shall consist of the temporary confinement of an offender in a penal facility or other location designated by the authorities.

**4.** Life imprisonment is an exceptional penalty consisting of the permanent confinement of an offender in a penal facility or other location designated by the authorities.

**5.** A total confiscation is an exceptional penalty consisting of the permanent seizure and transfer to the government of all or part of the property belonging to the offender as ordered by a court.
- (a) The court shall determine whether the Federal Government, Local Government, or both shall receive the seized property.

**6.** Exile is an exceptional penalty and shall be the compulsory removal of an offender from the territory of the Kingdom of Zatsunei and the prohibition from returning for a period determined by a court. 
- (a) Exile shall be enforced by banning the offender from accessing physical locations within the Kingdom of Zatsunei and official communications for the duration of the exile.

**7.** For the purposes of sentencing, penalties under this Code shall be divided into the following penalty categories:
- (a) Monetary penalties, consisting of a fine, an enhanced fine, and a maximum fine;

- (b) Custodial penalties, consisting of imprisonment and life imprisonment;

- (c) Community penalties, consisting of community service;

- (d) Property penalties, consisting of total confiscation;

- (e) Territorial penalties, consisting of exile.

## Chapter 4 ― Sentencing Provisions

### § 19. Sentencing principles
**1.** A sentence shall be determined according to the gravity of the criminal offense and the circumstances under which it was committed.

**2.** In determining a sentence, the court shall consider, where applicable:
- (a) the nature and gravity of the offense;

- (b) the harm or danger caused by the offense;

- (c) the intent, knowledge, or degree of negligence of the offender;

- (d) the role of the offender in the commission of the criminal offense;

- (e) the criminal record of the offender;

- (f) the conduct of the offender after the offence has been committed;

- (g) any aggravating or mitigating factors established by law.

**3.** Unless otherwise provided by this Code, a court may impose up to two penalties prescribed for the class of the offense, provided that no more than one penalty from each penalty category is imposed.

### § 20. Aggravating and mitigating circumstances
**1.** A court shall consider aggravating and mitigating circumstances when determining a sentence within the limits prescribed for the offense.

**2.** Aggravating circumstances shall include:
- (a) the offense was committed with particular cruelty or brutality;

- (b) the offense was committed against a vulnerable person;

- (c) the offense was committed as part of an organized or repeated criminal activity;

- (d) the offender intentionally caused substantially greater harm than was necessary to commit the criminal offense;

- (e) the offender has previously been convicted of a criminal offense

- (f) the offender has committed the same criminal offense multiple times.

**3.** Mitigating circumstances shall include:
- (a) the offender voluntarily attempted to prevent or reduce the harm caused by the criminal offense;

- (b) the offender acted under significant coercion, pressure, or other circumstances substantially reducing culpability;

- (c) the conduct of the offender has substantially caused less harm than generally associated with the offense;

- (d) the offender has no previous criminal convictions.


### § 21. Multiple offenses
**1.** Each commission of a criminal offense shall constitute one count of that offense.

**2.** Each additional count of the same criminal offense shall constitute an aggravating circumstance for sentencing, as prescribed under § 20(2)(f).

**3.** Counts of different criminal offenses shall be sentenced separately and consecutively.

**4.** A court shall consider the total number, circumstances, and nature of the counts when determining the final sentence.

### § 22. Sentencing limitations
**1.** A sentence shall remain within the penalty range prescribed for the applicable offense, unless otherwise provided by this Code.

**2.** Aggravating circumstances may increase a sentence within the penalty range but shall not permit the court to exceed the maximum penalty prescribed for the offense.

**3.** Mitigating circumstances may decrease a sentence within the penalty range but shall not permit the court to impose a penalty below the minimum prescribed for the offense.
