<br>
The Criminal Code establishes a fundamental list of crimes and basic regulations regarding criminal justice.
<br><br>
<p align="right">The Kingdom of Zatsunei</p> 
<hr>

<br>

# Title I ― General Provisions

## Chapter 1 ― Scope of Application

### § 1. No penalty before law
No act shall be punished unless it was defined as a criminal offense by law at the time it was
committed.

### § 2. Territorial validity
**1.** This Criminal Code is applicable to offenses committed within the territory of the Kingdom of Zatsunei.

**2.** The Criminal Code applies to Zatsunese land, airspace, and territorial waters.

### § 3. Validity on means of transport in foreign territory
The Criminal Code shall apply to means of transport under Zatsunese protection where such means of transport are identifiable as Zatsunese through visible markings or official documentation.

### § 4. Offenses in foreign territory
**1.** An offense committed in foreign territory against a Zatsunese national may be prosecuted under Zatsunese law when jurisdiction is established under this Code and the offender is subject to Zatsunese authority.

**2.** A Zatsunese national who commits an offense in foreign territory may be prosecuted under this Criminal Code when the person is subject to Zatsunese authority and was not punished in the foreign territory for the offense.

**3.** The Kingdom of Zatsunei may intervene against criminal offenses committed in foreign territory that threaten the security, sovereignty, or interests of Zatsunei.

<br>

## Chapter 2 ― Criminal Liability

### § 5. Definition and properties of an offense
**1.** An offense is an act contrary to law that is defined as punishable under this Criminal Code and for which criminal responsibility may arise.

**2.** An offense shall be evaluated according to the nature of the act, its legal classification, and its commission, including the place and time at which it occurred.

### § 6. Criminal responsibility
Any person who commits an act prohibited by this Criminal Code shall be held criminally responsible, unless otherwise provided by law.

### § 7. Mental incapacity
A person who commits an offense but has been officially deemed mentally incapable of understanding the criminal nature of the act shall not be punished or shall receive a significantly mitigated punishment.

### § 8. Intent and negligence
**1.** An intentional offense is committed when a person acts with knowledge and intent that their conduct will result in the commission of a criminal offense.

**2.** A negligent offense is committed when a person fails to exercise the required level of care, resulting in a criminal offense that is punishable under this Criminal Code, with the possibility of a mitigated sentence depending on the nature of the act.

### § 9. Attempts
Any attempt to commit a criminal offense shall be punishable in accordance with the nature and classification of the intended offense, with potential mitigation of sentence.

### § 10. Conspiracy
**1.** A person commits criminal conspiracy by intentionally agreeing with one or more persons to commit a criminal offense.

**2.** Criminal conspiracy shall be punishable by imprisonment for a term not exceeding one-half of the maximum penalty prescribed for the intended offense.
 
### § 11. REPEALED

### § 12. Withdrawal and abandonment
A person who voluntarily withdraws from the commission of a criminal offense and abandons the undertaking in good faith may receive a mitigated sentence or be exempt from punishment.

### § 13. Assistance against crime
**1.** A person who becomes aware of an imminent criminal offense shall make reasonable efforts to report or prevent it when reasonably able to do so.

**2.** A person who, through gross negligence, fails to make a reasonable effort to report or prevent the commission of a criminal offense commits a  Class B misdemeanor.

**3.** A person who withdraws from the commission of a criminal offense in accordance with § 12 and cooperates with the authorities to prevent or resolve the offense may receive further mitigation of punishment or may be exempt from punishment.

### § 14. Forms of offenders
The following forms of offenders shall be recognized under this Code:
- (a) Principal offender, an offender who directly commits a criminal offense;

- (b) Co-perpetrator, an offender who directly commits a criminal offense together with the principal offender or another co-perpetrator;

- (c) Instigator, an offender who intentionally causes, directs, or persuades another person to commit a criminal offense;

- (d) Accomplice, an offender who intentionally assists, facilitates, or provides the means for the commission or completion of a criminal offense.

### § 15. Corporate and collective criminal responsibility
**1.** A legal person may be held criminally responsible when an offense is committed by a person acting for, representing, or under the control of that legal person.

**2.** The responsibility of a legal person does not remove the responsibility of individuals involved in the offense.

**3.** Individuals who commit, assist, authorize, or fail to prevent an offense remain responsible under this Code.

**4.** Where a legal person is convicted of a criminal offense, only the following penalties may be imposed:
- (a) Monetary penalties;
- (b) Total confiscation, subject to the provisions established under § 18.8.

### § 16. Crimes committed by foreigners
A criminal offense committed by a foreign person may result in their deportation from the territory of the Kingdom of Zatsunei or other appropriate penalties in accordance with this Code.

<br>

## Chapter 3 ― Classification and Penalties of Offenses

### § 17. Classification of offenses
**1.** A misdemeanor is a criminal offense of lesser gravity and is classified into two classes:
- (a) Misdemeanor Class A, which is punishable by an enhanced fine or imprisonment for more than one week but not exceeding two weeks;

- (b) Misdemeanor Class B, which is punishable by a fine or imprisonment for more than three days but not exceeding one week;

**2.** A felony is a criminal offense of greater gravity and is classified into three classes:
- (a) Felony Class A, which is punishable by a maximum fine, imprisonment for more than five weeks but not exceeding eight weeks, or an exceptional penalty where authorized by this Code.

- (b) Felony Class B, which is punishable by a maximum fine or imprisonment for more than three weeks but not exceeding five weeks.

- (c) Felony Class C, which is punishable by an enhanced fine or imprisonment for more than two weeks but not exceeding three weeks.

### § 18. Penalties
**1.** A fine is a monetary penalty imposed on a person convicted of an offense. A fine may be imposed in the following forms:
- (a) Fine, which shall consist of the payment of 144 gold nuggets.

- (b) Enhanced fine, which shall consist of the payment of 288 gold nuggets.

- (c) Maximum fine, which shall consist of the payment of 576 gold nuggets.

- (d) Where an offender is sentenced to imprisonment and a fine, the fine shall become payable upon release. An offender who is unable to immediately pay the fine shall be granted a period of at least one week but not exceeding three weeks to obtain and pay the required amount.

- (e) Failure to pay a fine without reasonable justification constitutes a criminal offense punishable by imprisonment for more than two weeks but not exceeding three weeks.
  - (i) If an offender is unable to afford the fine, the court shall convert the monetary penalty into an equivalent period of community service.

**2.** Imprisonment shall consist of the temporary confinement of an offender in a penal facility or other location designated by the authorities.

**3.** Life imprisonment is an exceptional penalty consisting of the permanent confinement of an offender in a penal facility or other location designated by the authorities.

**4.** A total confiscation is an exceptional penalty consisting of the permanent seizure and transfer to the government of all or part of the property belonging to the offender as ordered by a court.
- (a) The court shall determine whether the Federal Government, Local Government, or both shall receive the seized property.

**5.** Exile is an exceptional penalty and shall be the compulsory removal of an offender from the territory of the Kingdom of Zatsunei and the prohibition from returning for a period determined by a court. 
- (a) Exile shall be enforced by banning the offender from accessing physical locations within the Kingdom of Zatsunei and official communications for the duration of the exile.

**6.** For the purposes of sentencing, penalties under this Code shall be divided into the following penalty categories:
- (a) Monetary penalties, consisting of a fine, an enhanced fine, and a maximum fine;

- (b) Custodial penalties, consisting of imprisonment and life imprisonment;

- (c) Property penalties, consisting of total confiscation;

- (d) Territorial penalties, consisting of exile;

- (e) Community penalties, consisting of community service.

**7.** Community service shall be the performance of unpaid work or services for the benefit of the public, as designated by the authorities.

**8.** An exceptional penalty may only be imposed for a Class A felony if the court explicitly finds that no other reasonable penalty sufficiently addresses the gravity of the offense.

<br>

## Chapter 4 ― Sentencing Provisions

### § 19. Sentencing principles
**1.** A sentence shall be determined according to the gravity of the criminal offense and the circumstances under which it was committed.

**2.** In determining a sentence, the court shall consider, where applicable:
- (a) the nature and gravity of the offense;

- (b) the harm or danger caused by the offense;

- (c) the intent, knowledge, or degree of negligence of the offender;

- (d) the role of the offender in the commission of the criminal offense;

- (e) the criminal record of the offender;

- (f) the conduct of the offender after the offense has been committed;

- (g) any aggravating or mitigating factors established by law.

### § 20. Aggravating and mitigating circumstances
**1.** A court shall consider aggravating and mitigating circumstances when determining a sentence within the limits prescribed for the offense.

**2.** Aggravating circumstances shall include:
- (a) the offense was committed with particular cruelty or brutality;

- (b) the offense was committed against a vulnerable person;

- (c) the offense was committed as part of an organized or repeated criminal activity;

- (d) the offender intentionally caused substantially greater harm than was necessary to commit the criminal offense.

**3.** Mitigating circumstances shall include:
- (a) the offender voluntarily attempted to prevent or reduce the harm caused by the criminal offense;

- (b) the offender acted under significant coercion, pressure, or other circumstances substantially reducing culpability;

- (c) the conduct of the offender has substantially caused less harm than generally associated with the offense;

- (d) the offender has no previous criminal convictions.

**4.** Aggravating circumstances shall justify a more severe sentence, and mitigating circumstances shall justify a less severe sentence.

### § 21. Multiple offenses and counts
**1.** Each distinct criminal offense for which the offender is convicted shall receive a separate sentence.

**2.** Multiple counts of the same criminal offense shall be treated as aggravating circumstances when determining the sentence for that offense.

**3.** Each sentence imposed for a separate criminal offense shall be enforced independently. Custodial penalties shall be served consecutively, provided that the total combined period of imprisonment imposed in a single proceeding does not exceed twice the maximum limit for the most severe charged crime.

**4.** The final sentence shall consist of all sentences imposed for the distinct criminal offenses.

### § 22. Sentencing limitations
**1.** A sentence shall remain within the penalty range prescribed for the applicable offense, unless otherwise provided by this Code.

**2.** A court may impose no more than one penalty from each penalty category and no more than two penalties in total.

**3.** Only penalties prescribed for the applicable offense may be imposed.

**4.** A sentence for a felony shall include at least one custodial penalty.

<br>

## Chapter 5 ― Defenses and Justifications

### § 23. Self-defense
**1.** A person who commits a criminal act solely to avert an imminent criminal threat to themselves or others shall not be considered guilty.

**2.** A person who exceeds the necessary level of self-defense due
to fright or fear shall not be held criminally responsible.

**3.** The act of self-defense must be the only reasonable and
proportionate method to neutralize the threat.

### § 24. Necessity
**1.** A person who commits a criminal act in order to avert an imminent and otherwise unavoidable danger to life, health, freedom, or significant property shall not be criminally responsible for that act.

**2.** The act shall be reasonably necessary to avert the danger and shall not cause harm subtantially greater than the harm it seeks to prevent.

### § 25. Lawful authority
**1.** A person shall not be criminally responsible for an act performed in accordance with lawful authority or a legal duty.

**2.** An act shall not be considered lawful when the person exceeds the authority or duty granted by law.

### § 26. Duress
**1.** A person who commits a criminal act under an immediate threat of death or serious bodily harm to themselves or another person shall not be considered criminally responsible if they could not reasonably avoid the threatened harm.

**2.** Duress shall not apply where the person voluntarily and intentionally placed themselves in a situation where duress was reasonably foreseeable.

### § 27. Mistake of fact
**1.** A person shall not be criminally responsible where, due to a mistake of fact, the person reasonably believed that their conduct was lawful.

**2.** Where the mistake resulted from negligence and the conduct constitutes a punishable negligent offense, the person shall be criminally responsible for that offense, as established under § 8.

### § 28. Mistake of law
Ignorance or misunderstanding of the law shall not excuse criminal responsibility.

<br>

## Chapter 6 ― Definitions and Miscellaneous

### § 29. Definitions
**1.** Authority shall refer to a person, institution, or body legally empowered to exercise governmental or official functions.

**2.** Means of transport or transport property shall refer to any object designed for or used in the transportation of persons or goods, including but not limited to motor vehicles, animals, aircraft, and water vessels

**3.** Subject to Zatsunese authority shall refer to any person who is physically present within Zatsunese territory or under jurisdiction of the Kingdom of Zatsunei.

**4.** A vulnerable person shall refer to a person who, due to age, disability, illness, dependence, or other significant circumstance, is substantially less able to protect themselves from harm and danger.

**5.** Property shall refer to anything capable of being legally owned, possessed, transferred, or otherwise controlled by a person or entity.
- (a) Significant property means property whose damage, loss, destruction, or deprivation causes substantial personal, financial, or operational harm to its owner or to the Kingdom.

**6.** Communications or official communications shall refer to online communication channels, digital platforms, and forums directly managed or officially endorsed by the Kingdom of Zatsunei.

**7.** State shall refer to the Kingdom of Zatsunei and her government, public institutions, authorities, territory, and her prefectures.

**8.** A deadly weapon shall refer to any object capable of causing death or serious physical harm when used against a person.

**9.** A Zatsunese national is a person who holds citizenship of the Kingdom of Zatsunei.

**10.** A public place refers to a place accessible to or used by the public, including private property open to public access.

**11.** Serious physical harm refers to physical harm involving a substantial risk of death, permanent injury, or a prolonged loss of a bodily function.

**12.** An official refers to a person holding a position within an authority, empowered to exercise governmental or official functions on its behalf.

**13.** A peace officer refers to a law enforcement official specifically empowered to enforce the law, make arrests, and maintain public order.

# Title II ― Criminal Offenses

## Chapter 1 ― Crimes against the State

### § 30. Treason
**1.** A person who intentionally assists a foreign power or enemy in a manner that seriously threatens the sovereignty, security, or constitutional order of the Kingdom or her prefectures commits treason.

**2.** Treason shall constitute a Class A felony and may warrant an exceptional penalty.

### § 31. Insurrection
**1.** A person who incites or participates in a rebellion or public uprising intended to undermine or seize control of the Federal or Local Government commits insurrection.

**2.** Insurrection shall constitute a Class A felony and may warrant an exceptional penalty.

### § 32. Desertion
**1.** A member of the Zatsunese Royal Defense Forces who deserts or abandons their military service during a time of war or national emergency is considered to have committed desertion.

**2.** Desertion shall constitute a Class A felony.

<br>

## Chapter 2 ― Crimes against the Person

### § 33. 1st degree murder
**1.** Whoever causes the death of another person with premeditation and malicious intent commits 1st degree murder.

**2.** 1st degree murder shall constitute a Class A felony.

### § 34. 2nd degree murder
**1.** Whoever causes the death of another person with malicious intent but lacks premeditation commits 2nd degree murder.

**2.** 2nd degree murder shall constitute a Class B felony.

### § 35. Manslaughter
**1.** A person who unintentionally causes the death of another person commits manslaughter.

**2.** Manslaughter shall constitute a Class C felony.

### § 36. Assault
**1.** A person who intentionally causes physical harm to another person without lawful justification commits assault.

**2.** Assault shall constitute a Class A misdemeanor.

### § 37. Aggravated assault
**1.** A person who intentionally causes serious physical harm to another person, or commits assault using a deadly weapon, commits aggravated assault.

**2.** Aggravated assault shall constitute a Class C felony.

### § 38. Unlawful restraint
**1.** Whoever wrongfully restrains, detains, or restricts the movement of another person against their will and with malicious intent commits unlawful restraint. 

**2.** Unlawful restraint shall constitute a Class C felony.

### § 39. Kidnapping
**1.** A person who forcibly abducts and holds another person captive commits kidnapping.

**2.** Kidnapping shall constitute a Class B felony.

### § 40. Hostage-taking
**1.** A person who takes or holds another person as a hostage with the intent to compel a person or other entity to act or refrain from acting commits hostage-taking.

**2.** Hostage-taking shall constitute a Class B felony.

### § 41. Criminal threats
**1.** A person who threatens another person with death or serious physical harm, intending to cause fear of imminent harm, is committing criminal threats.

**2.** Criminal threats shall constitute a Class A misdemeanor.

### § 42. Reckless endangerment
**1.** A person who recklessly engages in conduct that creates a substantial risk of death or serious physical harm to another person commits reckless endangerment.

**2.** Reckless endangerment shall constitute a Class A misdemeanor.

### § 43. Stalking
**1.** A person who maliciously and persistently follows another person and seeks to be near that person without their consent commits stalking.

**2.** Stalking shall constitute a Class A misdemeanor.

### § 44. Refusal to provide medical assistance
**1.** A person who intentionally and malevolently refuses to assist a person in immediate medical need, when capable of providing help, commits the refusal to provide medical assistance.

**2.** Refusal to provide medical assistance shall constitute a Class C felony.

**3.** If the refusal to provide medical assistance results in the death of the person in need, the offender shall be subject to a Class B felony.

### § 45. Coercion
**1.** A person who unlawfully forces or compels another person, through threats, intimidation, or psychological pressure, to act against their will commits coercion.

**2.** Coercion shall constitute a Class A misdemeanor.

### § 46. Person trafficking
**1.** Whoever recruits, transports, harbors, or receives a person for the purpose of exploitation, including forced labor or slavery, commits person trafficking.

**2.** Person trafficking shall constitute a Class A felony.

<br>

## Chapter 3 ― Crimes against Property

### § 47. Theft
**1.** A person who intentionally takes property belonging to another person or entity without their consent and with the intent to permanently deprive them of it commits theft.

**2.** Theft shall constitute a Class A misdemeanor.

### § 48. Aggravated theft
**1.** Whoever commits theft of significant property, or under circumstances of particular seriousness, commits aggravated theft.

**2.** Aggravated theft shall constitute a Class C felony.

### § 49. Robbery
**1.** Whoever takes property belonging to another person or entity by force or threat of force, with the intent to permanently deprive them of it, commits robbery.

**2.** Robbery shall constitute a Class C felony.

### § 50. Aggravated robbery
**1.** Whoever commits robbery under any of the following circumstances commits aggravated robbery:
- (a) The offender uses or displays a deadly weapon;

- (b) The offender causes serious physical harm to another person;

- (c) The offender is committing criminal threats during the robbery.

- (d) The offender is robbing significant property;

- (e) The offender acts together with two or more other persons;

- (f) The offender commits the offense against a vulnerable person.

**2.** Aggravated robbery shall constitute a Class B felony.

### § 51. Fraud
**1.** A person who dishonestly deceives another person and causes loss of property is committing fraud.

**2.** Fraud shall constitute a Class A misdemeanor.

**3.** In cases of particular seriousness, fraud may constitute a Class C felony.

### § 52. Damage to property
**1.** A person who unlawfully damages or alters property commits damage to property.

**2.** Damage to property shall constitute a Class A misdemeanor.

### § 53. Aggravated damage to property
**1.** A person who commits damage to property under any of the following circumstances commits aggravated damage to property:
- (a) The damage causes substantial financial loss;

- (b) The damage causes a substantial risk of death or serious physical harm to another person;

- (c) The damage is commited by fire, explosion, or another inherently dangerous means;

- (d) The damage is committed against critical infrastructure or property of significant public importance.

- (e) The property is in ownership of the Federal or Local Government.

**2.** Aggravated damage to property shall constitute a Class B felony.

### § 54. Trespassing
**1.** A person who intentionally enters or remains on property belonging to another person or entity without lawful authorization commits trespassing.

**2.** Trespassing shall constitute a Class A misdemeanor.

### § 55. Trespassing on governmental property
**1.** A person who intentionally enters or remains on property belonging to the Federal or Local Government without lawful authorization commits trespassing on governmental property.

**2.** Trespassing on governmental property shall constitute a Class C felony.

### § 56. Receiving stolen goods
**1.** A person who knowingly receives, acquires, conceals, or assists in the disposal of property obtained through criminal means commits receiving of stolen goods.

**2.** Receiving stolen goods shall constitute a Class C felony.

### § 57. Burglary
**1.** Whoever enters or remains on property belonging to another person or entity without lawful authorization, with the intent to commit a criminal offense on the property, commits burglary.

**2.** Burglary shall constitute a Class B felony.

### § 58. Embezzlement
**1.** A person who, having been lawfully entrusted with property belonging to another, unlawfully appropriates that property for their own use commits embezzlement.

**2.** Embezzlement shall constitute a Class A misdemeanor.

**3.** In cases involving significant property, embezzlement may constitute a Class C felony.

<br>

## Chapter 4 ― Crimes against Public Administration

### § 59. Perjury
**1.** A person who, having been lawfully sworn or affirmed to tell the truth before a court or other lawful proceeding, knowingly and intentionally makes a false statement to the proceeding commits perjury.

**2.** Perjury shall constitute a Class C felony.

### § 60. Obstruction of justice
**1.** Whoever intentionally interferes with, impedes, or prevents the lawful investigation, prosecution, or adjudication of a criminal offense commits obstruction of justice.

**2.** Obstruction of justice shall constitute a Class A misdemeanor.

**3.** Where obstruction of justice involves the destruction or concealment of evidence or tampering, it shall constitute a Class C felony.

### § 61. Bribery
**1.** A person who offers, promises, or provides any form of benefit, or who solicits or accepts any form of benefit, with the intent to influence the lawful performance of the duties of an official commits bribery.

**2.** Bribery shall constitute a Class C felony.

### § 62. Escape from custody
**1.** A person who flees from a lawful detainment or custody, or who fails to return to custody following a lawful temporary release, commits escape from custody.

**2.** Escape from custody shall constitute a Class C felony.

**3.** Whoever assists and enables another person in escaping from custody commits this offense, in accordance with § 14.

### § 63. Contempt of court
**1.** A person who disrespects, obstructs or disrupts the lawful proceedings of a court, or refuses to comply with court orders, commits contempt of court.

**2.** Contempt of court shall constitute a Class A misdemeanor.

**3.** If an offender is punished under this section, they shall not be punished under § 60.

### § 63a. Corruption
**1.** A government official or public official who abuses their position for personal gain or to grant an unlawful advantage to another person commits corruption.

**2.** Corruption shall constitute a Class B felony.
- (a) In severe cases of corruption, the offense shall constitute a Class A felony and may warrant an exceptional penalty.

<br>

## Chapter 5 ― Crimes against Public Order

### § 64. Rioting
**1.** Whoever participates with two or more persons in the use or threat of unlawful violence in a public place, causing another person to fear for their safety, commits rioting.

**2.** Rioting shall constitute a Class C felony.

### § 65. Unlawful assembly
**1.** A person who incites, organizes, or commands a gathering of persons for the purpose of committing unlawful acts or causing a public disturbance commits unlawful assembly.

**2.** Unlawful assembly shall constitute a Class A misdemeanor.

**3.** A person who participates in an unlawful assembly commits this offense, in accordance with § 14.

### § 66. Disorderly conduct
**1.** A person who, in a public place, engages in violent or disruptive behavior that causes a substantial disturbance to the public peace commits disorderly conduct.

**2.** Disorderly conduct shall constitute a Class B misdemeanor.

### § 67. Formation or participation in a criminal organization
**1.** Whoever establishes, leads, or organizes a group of three or more persons with the intent to commit criminal offenses or to undermine public safety commits formation of a criminal organization.

**2.** A person who knowingly joins or actively participates in such a group commits participation in a criminal organization.

**3.** Formation of a criminal organization shall constitute a Class B felony.

**4.** Participation in a criminal organization shall constitute a Class C felony.

**5.** Where the organization commits, or has committed, a Class A felony, or an offense involving serious violence, formation and participation under this section shall constitute a Class A felony and may warrant an exceptional penalty.

### § 68. Failure to comply
**1.** A person who resists or refuses to comply with lawful orders issued by a peace officer commits failure to comply.

**2.** Failure to comply shall constitute a Class B misdemeanor.

### § 69. Terrorism
**1.** A person who commits or threatens a severe violent act against persons, property, environment, or infrastructure, with the intent to intimidate a person or compel a government or entity to act or refrain from acting commits terrorism.

**2.** Terrorism shall constitute a Class A felony and may warrant an exceptional penalty.
